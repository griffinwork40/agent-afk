I gave an AI agent full control of my computer. It cloned itself 134 times.

Not a cyberattack. Not a glitch. The agent was trying to figure out what it could do -- and the system never told it no. Each copy launched another copy. Within seconds my laptop was drowning in AI agents, all politely trying to be helpful.

I build autonomous AI agents that work while you're away from your keyboard. After this incident, I rewrote the rules that govern how they operate. Three things I landed on:

1. Act freely on anything you can undo. Stop and ask before anything you can't. The line isn't importance -- it's reversibility.

2. Every task ends in a declared outcome: Done, Blocked, Asking, or Interrupted. No vague status updates. When you check back in, you know exactly what happened.

3. Clean principles aren't enough by themselves. You also need ugly, specific rules born from things that actually went wrong. Same reason buildings need both architectural standards and fire codes.
